Field guide

Trades card editor

One HTML file that turns a raw chart screenshot into a clean 1920 × 1080 trade card — session tabs, symbol badge, timeframe pill, wordmark and your own corner badge — and exports it at up to 5760 × 3240.

Whyit can promise that

The preview canvas is the export canvas

Most editors keep a fast preview and a separate render path for the final file. The two drift — a label lands a pixel left, a shadow renders heavier, and the download never quite matches what you framed.

Here the on-screen canvas is declared at 1920 × 1080 and the export reads that same composition back. There is no second render path that can disagree with the first, so the downloaded PNG is the thing you looked at.

Good to know

The composition is always 1920 × 1080 — a fixed 16:9 frame. Export quality changes how many pixels that composition is rasterised into, never how it is laid out. A card framed at 1080p and a card framed at 6K are the same card.

Privacy

No server component. Nothing is stored, no cookies are set, and no data is sent anywhere. The only network request the page makes is for its two webfonts.

01Workflow

Get it in, then frame it

Framing is the part that takes the longest and matters the most.

Three ways in

Load screenshot opens the file picker, you can drop a file anywhere on the canvas, or press Ctrl / ⌘ V anywhere on the page. Anything the browser can decode works — PNG, JPG, WebP, GIF, AVIF.

What lands

The original is kept at full resolution. Past 3840 px on the long edge a lighter working copy is built and the editor drives off that, which is why dragging a 6000 px screenshot still feels immediate — and it costs nothing, because export reads the pixels back out of the original.

And resets

Zoom to 100%, offset to centre, all four trims to zero, fit mode to Fit. Your labels are untouched.

HEIC will not load

Browsers cannot decode Apple's HEIC format. On iPhone, either take a screenshot — those are always PNG — or switch the camera over in Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible.

ThenFrame it
Fit / Fill

Where framing starts, and Fit to begin with. Fit shows the whole screenshot and pads the leftover edges with the card background; Fill covers the frame edge to edge and lets the overflow crop away. Either one resets zoom and position.

Zoom

Drag the slider (20–400%), or step with ±0.1% and ±0.5% for the last bit of alignment. Scroll or pinch on the canvas zooms toward the pointer and reaches 600%.

Position

Drag the canvas, nudge 1 px or 10 px with the buttons, or use the arrow keys — Shift makes each press 10 px. Centre returns the offset to 0, 0 without touching zoom.

Reset

Zoom, position, all four trims and fit mode go back to defaults. Your screenshot and every label survive it.

Remove screenshot

Clears the image and its framing, and frees the memory it was holding. Labels and overlay settings survive that too.

Good to know

Hold Shift while scrolling and the zoom step drops from 2% to 0.2% per notch. It is the difference between hunting for an alignment and landing on it.

Trim edges

The control that solves half candles. Zooming to hide a half-drawn candle at the right edge moves everything else too. Trim works differently: it crops in source pixels, per side, before the frame is computed — so the edge goes away and the composition stays put.

Controls

Each side has −, + and +10, and a box you can type into. With the cursor in that box, ↑ and ↓ step the trim 1 px — these are the only arrow keys in the editor that trim rather than move. Opposite sides cannot meet — at least 20 px of image always survives.

02Workflow

Choose the ground

Every colour on the card derives from it — which is why it comes before the labels, not after.

Six presets, a picker and a hex field, starting at #F5F5F5. Pick anything else and every part of the overlay re-derives itself rather than leaving dark type stranded on a dark card.

Glass

Drawn from the background, so it keeps the same relationship to it at every colour.

Type

Keeps a trace of the background's hue instead of snapping to pure black or white, and flips to light once the ground is dark. Every preset clears 14:1.

Session pill & edges

The pill inverts on dark grounds; hairlines and shadows swap with it. That is what keeps a panel legible once the card goes black.

ThenHow far off it

Overlay tone

The control most worth learning, because a bright chart and a dark chart want opposite answers. Tone moves the glass relative to the ground — up toward white, or down into a soft grey. Default +50.

The range is read off whichever ground you picked. Pure white is the only one with no room above it — there the separation comes from below instead, so the glass never dissolves into the card.

Good to know

Tone moves fill and opacity together, and each element keeps its own weight — the symbol capsule always reads stronger than the rail behind it, the timeframe pill stronger than both. You cannot knock the three out of balance.

Overlay size

Scales every piece of furniture together, 55–130%, from 100%. Tabs, badge, capsule, pill, corner badge and wordmark keep their proportions to each other — one number, no drift.

The bottom of that range is genuinely small. It exists because a busy chart wants the overlay to get out of the way, and a slider that only ever trims a tenth cannot do that.

Two numbers

The panel reads 55% · type 73%, and it means it. The footprint takes the full setting. The type travels at 60% of that, because a label scaled to 55% on a 1080px card is four grey pixels on a phone. Boxes take whichever is larger — their own size, or what the label inside them needs — so at the bottom of the range the pill hugs its text instead of crushing it.

At exactly 100% the two agree to the pixel, so a card built before any of this looks the same as it always did.

Rail width

The bottom bar answers the size slider two ways, and which one is right depends on the card rather than on the number. Full pins it to the card — 16px off each edge, however small the furniture inside gets — so a quiet chart keeps a plinth running its whole width. Fitted lets it draw in with everything else, at 55% covering 55% of the width it covered at 100%, so a busy chart gets the bar out of the way instead of wearing a thinner version of the same stripe.

At 100% they are the same bar to the pixel. The choice only starts to mean anything once you move off it.

03Workflow

Label it, then sign it

Set once. All of it survives the next screenshot, so a run of cards only pays for this here.

Symbol

Seven instruments. Every badge is drawn on the canvas rather than loaded as an image — which is why they stay sharp at 6K, and why the index futures carry the index size instead of the ticker. Starts on NQ.

Session

London, New York, Asia. Whichever is active is always pinned to the centre of the card; the other two rotate around it and fade outward, so the eye lands on the one that matters.

Drag it. At 0 the two inactive labels sit flat grey; raising it dissolves each one toward its outer end, away from the centre.

Timeframe

Ten presets, or type up to six characters — uppercased as you go. It lands in the pill above the rail and in the exported filename.

M1M3M5 M15M30H1 H4H6D1 W1
ThenSign it
Logo

X or Instagram, both drawn as vectors at export time, so neither softens at 6K.

Username

Up to 20 characters, case preserved — unlike the timeframe field — so a handle like yser.trades stays lowercase.

Show on card

Hides the wordmark. The rail, badge and pill are untouched.

Corner badge

Upload your own image and it sits at the right end of the bottom rail, opposite the symbol and exactly its size — the two ends of the bar become a matched pair. Fitted inside the square rather than cropped to fill it, so a wide mark stays wide and none of it is lost. Transparent PNG sits best. Remove takes it off again.

Good to know

Clear the username entirely and the logo centres on its own, rather than leaving a dangling slash. A bare mark is often the cleaner signature.

The badge is kept at full size

The uploaded image is held at its original resolution and redrawn from that at every export, the same way your screenshot is. Upload it large — a 512px mark carries a 6K card, a 64px favicon will not.

04Workflow

Nothing is upscaled

Lossless PNG. Three sizes. Every one redrawn at its own resolution, never stretched.

1080p1920 × 1080Drawn at 3840, resolved down
4K3840 × 2160Drawn at 7680 · default
6K5760 × 3240Drawn at 11520

What actually happens at 6K

Every label, badge and panel is drawn again at the output size rather than stretched from the preview, and the image pixels come from your original screenshot, not the editing copy.

Each size is also rendered at twice its own width and resolved back down, which is what actually smooths the curve of a badge and the edge of the type. Every size gets that full double pass — picking the biggest one no longer trades edge quality for pixels.

If the device refuses a canvas that large the export steps down its own quality — one and a half times, then a plain native redraw — rather than giving up. You get the best pass the phone in your hand can actually hold, and the file either way.

Your screenshot is the ceiling

That cleans up the overlay, not the chart. A 1080p screenshot exported at 6K gives crisp badges over a chart with no more detail than it started with. Grab the source large and the big sizes pay off.

Filenames

Exports name themselves from what is on the card: NQ-NewYork-H4-3840x2160.png. Sort a folder of them and they group by instrument on their own.

Getting the file out

A download fires automatically, then a bar appears with a thumbnail and three routes. This is deliberate: inside an embedded frame a plain download can be blocked by the host's sandbox, and at least one of these always works.

Download

Fires the download again, by hand.

Share

The native share sheet. Appears only where the device supports sharing files — mostly phones.

Open in tab

Opens the PNG on its own so you can save it manually. The universal fallback.

If a device cannot allocate a canvas that large, the export steps down and tells you to pick a smaller size rather than failing silently.

ReferenceInput

Without touching the panel

Every key, every gesture — and the one exception that trims instead of moves.

Keyboard

Ctrl / ⌘ VPaste a screenshot from the clipboard
← → ↑ ↓Move the image 1 px. They move it — they never trim.
Shift + arrowMove 10 px
Shift + scrollFine zoom — 0.2% a notch instead of 2%
↑ ↓ in a trim boxStep that side's trim 1 px. The one exception below.

While the cursor is in a field the arrows stop moving the frame, so typing a custom timeframe or a username never shifts your screenshot. The four trim boxes are the exception, and only because they hold numbers: ↑ and ↓ there step that edge 1 px, and Shift makes no difference — it is still 1. Click off the box and the arrows go back to moving the image.

Good to know

Lift one finger mid-pinch and the gesture becomes a drag from exactly where that finger sits — no jump. Zoom and pan are one continuous motion.

The sixty-second card

Once it is muscle memory, this is the whole loop.

  1. Snip the chartCtrl / ⌘ Shift S on TradingView copies the chart straight to the clipboard. Or snip with the OS — Win Shift S, or ⌘ Shift 4 on a Mac. Windows puts that on the clipboard; a Mac saves it to the desktop unless you add Ctrl.
  2. PasteCtrl / ⌘ V anywhere on the page.
  3. Fill, then trimHit Fill, then trim the edge with the half candle. Two clicks, no drift.
  4. Set the groundBright chart, push tone negative. Dark chart, leave it lifted. Every overlay colour follows from here.
  5. Set the labelsSymbol, session, timeframe — and your logo, handle and corner badge, once. All of it persists between screenshots.
  6. Export4K is the default and the right answer almost always.
Remember

What you see in the preview is the file. If the card looks right on screen, it is right in the download — there is no second render that can disagree.
